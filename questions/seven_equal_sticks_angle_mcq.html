The Bamboo Trellis Puzzle

Read the story, study the figure, then answer the four questions. Each question locks after your first choice.

It is the first Sunday of the monsoon in Bulandshahr, and Aarav is helping his Dada ji build a trellis for the bean vines in the backyard. Dada ji has already fixed two long wooden rails into the ground. They meet at a single corner, A, and spread apart like the two arms of a V lying on its side.

“Now the clever part,” says Dada ji, handing Aarav a bundle. Inside are seven bamboo sticks, every one of them cut to exactly the same length. “Lay the first stick along the lower rail, from the corner A to B. Then zig-zag: from B go up to the upper rail at C, from C back down to the lower rail at D, from D up to E, from E down to F, and from F up to G.”

Aarav does exactly that. Six sticks are in place, each one touching the rails at both ends. He picks up the seventh stick, lays it along the upper rail from G towards the corner, and it fits perfectly, ending exactly at A.

Dada ji smiles. “That was no accident. Only one angle at the corner lets all seven equal sticks fit like this. Tell me, Aarav, what is the angle ∠EAD at the corner?”

AB = BCBC = CDCD = DEDE = EFEF = FGFG = GA
Let ∠EAD = α. The labels show angles in terms of α.
Question 1Warm-up

Let ∠EAD = α. In triangle ABC, AB = BC. What is the size of the exterior angle ∠CBD at B?

In ΔABC, AB = BC, so the angles opposite these equal sides are equal: ∠BCA = ∠BAC = α. The exterior angle at B equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles, so ∠CBD = α + α = 2α.
Question 2Climbing the chain

Using the same idea stick by stick, what is the size of the exterior angle ∠EFD at F (the angle between stick EF and the lower rail, on the side of D)?

  1. In ΔAGF, AG = GF, so ∠AFG = ∠GAF = α. The exterior angle at G is ∠FGE = 2α.
  2. In ΔGFE, GF = FE, so ∠FEG = ∠FGE = 2α. That is, ∠AEF = 2α.
  3. In ΔAEF, the exterior angle at F is ∠EFD = ∠EAF + ∠AEF = α + 2α = 3α.
Question 3Dada ji's question

What is the angle ∠EAD at the corner of the trellis?

Collect the angles of ΔEFD:
  1. ∠EFD = 3α (Question 2).
  2. EF = ED, so ∠EDF = ∠EFD = 3α.
  3. In ΔCAD, the exterior angle at C is ∠DCE = α + 2α = 3α (because ∠CDA = ∠CBD = 2α, as CB = CD). Since DC = DE, ∠DEC = ∠DCE = 3α. Then ∠FED = ∠DEC − ∠FEC = 3α − 2α = α.
  4. Angle sum of ΔEFD: α + 3α + 3α = 180°, so 7α = 180° and α = 180°⁄7 ≈ 25.7°.
The choices 20°, 30° and 36° would need 9, 6 or 5 equal sticks, not 7.
Question 4Bonus

Next week Dada ji builds a smaller trellis with the same zig-zag pattern but only five equal sticks, again ending exactly at the corner. What must the corner angle be?

Each new stick adds one more α to the base angle of the next isosceles triangle: α, 2α, 3α, … With n equal sticks the closing triangle always gives nα = 180°, so the corner angle is 180°⁄n. For n = 5 that is 180°⁄5 = 36°. (With 7 sticks it was 180°⁄7, matching Question 3.)
